Round 15 · Pre-race deep dive

Baku: track, tyres, incidents & the Antonelli call

Azerbaijan Grand Prix — written the morning after qualifying, before the race.
Track character
Baku’s clipping severity (full-throttle energy demand under the 2026 regs) comes out at 0.073 — mid-pack among the 12 circuits already gated this season, not the extreme high-speed outlier its reputation suggests. The tight castle section dilutes what the long straights would otherwise demand. Year-on-year vs 2025 at the same circuit: every team lost time, but Mercedes lost the least (1.07s), Aston Martin the most (4.74s) — consistent with Russell taking pole.
Tyre strategy
All three dry compounds nominated. Practice long-run pace (push laps only, filtering the alternating push/cooldown pattern most drivers ran through their stints): Soft degrades fastest, Medium next, Hard lowest — see the pace ladder’s long-run view for the exact numbers per driver. 2025’s race here was a 1-stop for 17 of 20 finishers (Medium → Hard the dominant pattern); a second stop only becomes “free” opportunistically behind a Safety Car.
Safety Car / VSC / red flag risk
The standout number. Every one of the last 5 Baku races (2021–2025) had a Safety Car or Virtual Safety Car deployed — 2021 also had a genuine red flag (Perez/Stroll wall impact). Against this season’s overall base rate (SC in 7 of 14 races, zero VSCs, zero red flags in 2026 so far), Baku is running well above the season’s incident rate. That cuts both ways: more overtaking upside when the field bunches up, more DNF/damage risk on a wall-lined street circuit.
Antonelli qualified P16 (Q1 out) — captain on both your teams
Grid-conditioned model (built specifically for this call — see predict.points.build_grid_conditioned_distributions in the repo): even from P16, Antonelli still projects as the #1 expected scorer in the field (mean 39.1, next best Russell at 24.9), because his rolling-form strength ranks #1 of 23 drivers this season and Baku’s 1-stop, high-incident profile rewards a fast car starting mid-pack. Real spread as your captain (2x): roughly 4 to 128 points (10th–90th percentile) — a genuine bet, not a safe one, and the model has no way to know why he was knocked out in Q1.

Verdict: don’t burn Final Fix to bench him. The math doesn’t support dropping your highest projected scorer over one bad grid slot, especially at a track this good for a recovery drive. If the captain 2x variance specifically is the worry, check whether captaincy alone can still be swapped for free before lights out — that de-risks the multiplier without spending your last chip.
Follow-up: Norris → Verstappen?
Doesn’t clear the bar on any signal checked: Norris starts three grid slots ahead (P5 vs P8), projects marginally higher for the race (18.2 vs 17.1), ranks fractionally ahead on rolling season form (#5 vs #6), outscored Verstappen over the last 4 rounds (avg 27.8 vs 14.5, or 24.7 excluding Verstappen’s round-12 DNF), and costs less ($27.0M vs $27.3M). If you want to upgrade that slot anyway, Hamilton and Leclerc both outrank Norris on the model right now and cost less than either driver above.
