Round 16 · Pre-race deep dive

Sepang: why the Bahrain GP is in Malaysia, and what we can and can't say yet

Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia — written race week, before Friday practice has run.
Why this race is where it is
F1 and the FIA cancelled the original April Bahrain and Saudi Arabian rounds outright because of the 2026 Iran war, cutting the season to 22 races. Bahrain's round was later reinstated for 4 October — not at Sakhir, but at the Sepang International Circuit in Malaysia, with Bahrain still footing the bill and the event still officially named the Bahrain Grand Prix. Saudi Arabia's round has not been rescheduled. This is Sepang's first F1 race since the 2017 Malaysian Grand Prix.
Track character
5.543km, run clockwise, 15 corners and 8 straights (two of them long, high-speed) on an unusually wide track (16–22m) designed by Hermann Tilke — sweeping, wide-radius corners rather than a tight street-circuit layout, closer in character to Baku's straights than to its walls. The asphalt is genuinely abrasive and, per Pirelli, has not been resurfaced since that 2017 race — a real, verified factor for tyre wear this weekend, not a guess.

What this project can't say yet: the clipping-severity and year-on-year pace numbers on the Baku deep-dive two rounds ago came from this project's own telemetry analysis (pace/track_profile.py), which needs a recent, modern lap of the circuit to work from. FastF1's telemetry/lap API doesn't cover Sepang's last F1 visit (2017 predates it), and this weekend's own practice hasn't run yet — so there is no real lap to build that number from. It'll be possible once FP1 or FP2 laps exist.
Tyre strategy
Pirelli has nominated the C2 / C3 / C4 compounds — the mid-range trio, not the softest set it used for the last street-circuit rounds. Pirelli's own stated reasoning: Sepang sits at medium tyre stress on their cross-track scale, but that worn, abrasive, un-resurfaced asphalt pushes wear up regardless, and avoiding the hardest compounds keeps a one-stop and a two-stop race close enough in lap time to leave strategy genuinely open, rather than the race being decided by pit wall math alone.
Weather
Sepang's tropical climate was a recurring theme across its 1999–2017 F1 history — sudden afternoon storms strong enough to red-flag a session were common enough to become part of the circuit's reputation. That's long-run climate context, not a specific forecast for this race weekend, and this project doesn't have a weather feed to make it one.
